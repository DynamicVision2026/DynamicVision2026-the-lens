# Pain evidence: 「テスト前、何を勉強すればいいかわからない」

Research date: 2026-10-18. Method: WebSearch plus WebFetch of each page. Only pages actually read are counted. Japanese quotes are kept under 15 characters.

**Access limits (affects the counts):**
- Yahoo!知恵袋's search page is robots-blocked. Individual question pages could be read until the proxy rate-limited them (HTTP 429) after about 25 fetches.
- ママスタ, note.com search and X/Twitter could not be searched directly.
- The search engine mostly returns juku SEO articles, not posts from parents and students.

The evidence below is therefore weighted toward 知恵袋, inter-edu (エデュナビ) parent Q&A, and parent blogs (Ameba, はてな, note).

---

## Verdict

**Moderate overall. Strong for one segment and one variant.**

1. **The macro problem is real, large and growing.** In the 東大社研・ベネッセ「子どもの生活と学びに関する親子調査2022」, 61.1% of 小4–6 and 68.1% of 中学生 agree with 「上手な勉強のしかたがわからない」. The figure rose every year from 2019 to 2022, by +18.5 points for elementary and +8.1 points for junior high. This is a survey fact, not a post count.

2. **(c) is the strongest and most product-shaped variant: making a 間違いノート is manual labour.** This pain is concentrated in **中学受験 families**. Parents describe:
   - photocopying, cutting and pasting wrong problems into a 間違い直しノート
   - calling it 「すごくめんどう」 and saying it costs 「時間と労力」
   - building DIY workarounds with Adobe Scan, Goodnotes, Anki or handwriting-erasing apps

   That DIY behaviour is the clearest sign of willingness to pay. It maps almost exactly onto "snap graded work → organised review list".

3. **(d) is the loudest variant: parent burden and fights.** But parents frame it as motivation and how much to intervene: 「口出しすぎ」, 「どこまで関与」, 「イライラ」, 「疲れてしまいました」, 「伴走」, 「つきっきり」. They do not frame it as "we don't know what to review". A tool helps with (d) only indirectly, by letting the parent step back.

4. **(a), not knowing what to review, appears mostly as *students* on 知恵袋 asking 「テスト勉強の仕方がわからない」 or 「範囲が広すぎて何から」.** These are generic how-to-study questions. Very few posts specifically say "the night before, I don't know *which* problems to review". Parents almost never use the phrase 「何を勉強すればいいかわからない」 about themselves.

5. **(b), mistakes scattered or notebook chaos, is under-voiced.** It shows up as:
   - 「点数だけ見て終わってしまう」 and 「次も同じミスをする」, which a juku reports as common parent consultations
   - one 中1 parent who threw away corrected sheets and so couldn't track repeated errors

   It is real, but parents don't name it as a problem on its own.

6. **Base rate check.** In a random snapshot of 30 posts under the Ameba tag 「定期テスト」, only 2 touched this pain, and both were about parents studying alongside the child. Public-school families do not talk about this constantly.

**Implication:** The pain is real. The sharpest, most monetisable version is 中受 parents who already do mistake-notebook work by hand. For public 中学 定期テスト, the need exists (6–7 in 10 students don't know how to study well), but parents describe it as 「勉強のやり方がわからない」 and 「親はどこまで?」, not as a review-list problem. Marketing copy should use their words (see next section).

**Gap summary:** No widely adopted Japanese product takes photos of a child's *own graded school tests and prints*, maps each mistake to the 教科書/学習指導要領 unit, and outputs a list filtered by the upcoming テスト範囲. GakuMate is the nearest and uses almost the same pitch, but it has negligible traction. Big players (スタディサプリ, 進研ゼミ, Qubena) map units only within their own content. Photo-solvers (QANDA) and handwriting-erasers solve single steps of the workflow.
